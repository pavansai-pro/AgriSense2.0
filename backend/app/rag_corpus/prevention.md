# Crop health prevention guide

## Acidic soil (low pH)
- Apply agricultural lime (calcium carbonate) 2-4 weeks before sowing; typical dose 1-2 tonnes per hectare, confirm with a soil test.
- Use dolomite lime if magnesium is also low.
- Add well-decomposed farmyard manure or compost to buffer pH.
- Avoid over-using ammonium-based fertilizers such as urea and ammonium sulphate, which acidify soil.

## Alkaline or saline soil (high pH, salinity)
- Apply gypsum (calcium sulphate) as per soil test, then irrigate to leach salts below the root zone.
- Elemental sulphur or pressmud lowers pH slowly over a season.
- Improve drainage so salts do not build up; avoid saline irrigation water where possible.
- Choose tolerant crops or varieties (barley, cotton, some wheat varieties) on salt-affected fields.

## Nitrogen deficiency
- Symptoms: older leaves turn pale yellow first, plants look stunted.
- Split nitrogen: apply part at sowing and the rest at tillering, flowering or earthing up.
- Use neem-coated urea to reduce losses; add green manure or legumes in rotation.
- Do not apply nitrogen just before heavy rain; it washes away.

## Excess nitrogen
- Too much nitrogen causes soft, lush growth that attracts pests and lodging.
- Skip the next nitrogen top-dressing and balance with potassium.
- Keep spacing wide enough for air movement to reduce disease.

## Phosphorus deficiency
- Symptoms: purple or dark leaves, weak roots, late maturity.
- Apply DAP or single super phosphate at sowing, placed near the root zone.
- Use phosphate-solubilising bacteria (PSB) bio-fertilizer with organic manure.

## Potassium deficiency
- Symptoms: leaf edges turn brown and scorched, weak stems, poor grain or fruit filling.
- Apply muriate of potash (MOP) or sulphate of potash; split doses in sandy soils.
- Potassium improves drought and disease tolerance, so do not skip it before stress periods.

## Waterlogging and heavy rain
- Open field drains and ridges before forecast heavy rain; do not let water stand more than 24-48 hours.
- Postpone fertilizer and pesticide sprays when heavy rain is expected within 24 hours.
- Raised beds or ridge-and-furrow sowing protect roots of maize, cotton, tomato and pulses.
- After the rain, apply a light nitrogen dose to help recovery and watch for root rot.

## Water stress and dry spells
- Irrigate in the early morning or evening; drip irrigation saves 30-50% water.
- Mulch with crop residue or plastic to keep soil moisture.
- Spray 1-2% potassium nitrate or apply potassium to improve drought tolerance.
- Prioritise irrigation at critical stages: flowering and grain or fruit filling.
- Harvest rainwater in farm ponds for protective irrigation.

## Heat stress
- Irrigate lightly and more often during heat waves to cool the canopy.
- Mulching and shade nets protect vegetables like tomato.
- Avoid spraying chemicals in the hottest hours.
- Adjust sowing dates so flowering does not fall in the hottest weeks.

## Cold stress and frost
- Light irrigation in the evening before a frosty night keeps fields warmer.
- Smoke or cover nurseries and young vegetable plants on cold nights.
- Spray sulphur-based products or potassium to improve cold tolerance in sensitive crops like potato and tomato.

## Fungal disease, blight and high humidity
- Warm, humid weather (humidity above 85%) favours blight, blast, rust and mildew.
- Inspect leaves twice a week; remove and destroy infected plants.
- Use preventive sprays such as mancozeb or copper oxychloride before long humid spells, following label doses.
- Keep good spacing, avoid excess nitrogen, and do not irrigate in the evening.
- Use disease-resistant varieties and certified seed treated with fungicide or Trichoderma.

## Pest outbreaks
- Install pheromone traps and yellow sticky traps to monitor pests early.
- Spray neem oil (1500 ppm) at the first sign of sucking pests.
- Encourage natural enemies; avoid broad-spectrum insecticides unless pest levels cross thresholds.
- Contact your Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) for pest identification.

## Harvest rain and post-harvest storage
- If rain is forecast around harvest, harvest a few days early when grain is mature, or delay until a dry window.
- Dry grain to safe moisture (about 12-14%) before storage; use tarpaulins on drying yards.
- Store in clean, dry, raised bags or hermetic bags to avoid mould and pests.

## Early harvest
- Harvesting before physiological maturity lowers yield and quality.
- Check maturity signs: grain hardness, colour change, or fruit firmness, before cutting.

## Late harvest
- Delayed harvest causes grain shattering, lodging, and pest damage in the field.
- Plan labour and machinery in advance so harvest happens in the ideal window.

## Crop insurance
- Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY) covers yield loss from natural risks; enrol through your bank, CSC or the PMFBY portal before the cut-off date.
- Restructured Weather Based Crop Insurance Scheme (RWBCIS) pays out on weather triggers like excess rain or heat.
- Report crop loss within 72 hours of a local calamity to claim insurance.
