# Crop guides

## Rice cultivation tips
- Grows best in clayey or loamy soil that holds water, pH 5.5-6.5.
- Transplant 20-25 day old seedlings; keep 2-5 cm standing water during tillering.
- Common risks: blast and bacterial leaf blight in humid weather, stem borer, brown plant hopper.
- Drain the field 10-15 days before harvest.

## Wheat cultivation tips
- Sow in November for best yield in north India; late sowing reduces yield.
- Critical irrigation at crown root initiation (about 21 days after sowing), flowering and grain filling.
- Common risks: yellow rust in cool humid weather, terminal heat stress in March.

## Maize cultivation tips
- Needs well-drained soil; very sensitive to waterlogging.
- Apply nitrogen in three splits: sowing, knee-high stage and tasselling.
- Common risks: fall armyworm (check whorls), stalk rot after heavy rain.

## Potato cultivation tips
- Cool season crop; tubers form best at 15-20°C.
- Earth up 25-30 days after planting; keep soil moist but not wet.
- Common risks: late blight in cool, humid, cloudy weather; spray preventive fungicide.

## Tomato cultivation tips
- Use staking and mulching; transplant 25-30 day old seedlings.
- Calcium and even watering prevent blossom-end rot.
- Common risks: early and late blight, leaf curl virus spread by whitefly, fruit borer.

## Sugarcane cultivation tips
- Long-duration crop (10-12 months); needs plenty of water and nutrients.
- Use healthy setts treated with fungicide; trash mulching conserves moisture.
- Common risks: red rot, early shoot borer, waterlogging in heavy monsoon.

## Cotton cultivation tips
- Grows well in black soils; sensitive to waterlogging.
- Monitor pink bollworm with pheromone traps; destroy crop residue after harvest.
- Avoid excess nitrogen which encourages sucking pests.

## Soybean cultivation tips
- Treat seed with Rhizobium culture; needs little nitrogen fertilizer.
- Sow on broad bed furrows to handle both heavy rain and dry spells.
- Common risks: yellow mosaic virus, girdle beetle, waterlogging.

## Groundnut cultivation tips
- Needs light, sandy loam soil; apply gypsum at flowering for pod filling.
- Common risks: leaf spot and rust in humid weather, white grub, aflatoxin if pods are stored wet.
